# idea-machine

Humans are working at breakneck speed to develop AI, despite the increasing risk that it will lead to their obsolescence (in terms of performing useful work), and potentially extinction. Various explanations are given for the accelerating pursuit of AI despite these concerns: supercharging scientific discovery, improving productivity, liberation from drudge work, etc. However, all explanations are generally backstopped by a different rationale: if we don't build it someone else, e.g. a geopolitical adversary, will. This casts AI as an inevitability despite the deleterious, potentially catastrophic, risks readily acknowledged by its builders.

Consider the human species as a single entity with the following characteristics:
- It's composed of a distributed network of semi-autonomous sub-units.
- Its sole motivating principle is self-propagation/perpetuation.
- It requires a continuous intake of negentropy to persist and grow.
- It channels its negentropy resources in service of the above via an explore/exploit strategy:
  - Exploit: produce more sub-units
  - Explore: develop new means of acquiring negentropy, harden itself against internal and external threats.

 Question:
 - Does an entity as described above naturally trend towards a state of higher information density, e.g. a group of humans -> AI?
